Two tabs, one space

Second tab in the same space · real HTTP response from 127.0.0.1

This page exists so the tab strip has something ordinary in it: a second document, its own title, its own scroll position, loaded in the same space as the storage probe next door.

What the shell is doing here

Every tab gets its own CefBrowser parented into a drawing area that is pinned to the X root visual. The tab strip, sidebar and toolbar are plain GTK3 widgets; the page is Chromium 156, unmodified.

Spaces are the product. Tabs are the thing you actually click.
  • Switching tabs hides one browser and shows the other (WasHidden keeps parked tabs cheap).
  • Switching spaces swaps the whole strip; each space owns a request context.
  • Closing the last tab in a space gives you a new tab page, not a broken window.

What it does not do yet

No drag-to-reorder, no tab tearing, no find-in-page, no history menu. The bridge that gives agents their bindings lands in M2 behind the ControlServer seam; this build only wires the delegate.

Rendered by CEF 156. If you can read this, the GTK drawing area is carrying a real compositor surface, not a screenshot.
